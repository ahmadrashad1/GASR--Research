Mid Evaluation · FYP-2

The methodology, finalised

Given the last 4 frames of 512 tracked tissue points, predict their displacement k frames ahead, k ∈ {1, 2, 3}. No clinical labels, anywhere.

Model

  • Conditional denoising diffusion
  • v-prediction, cosine schedule
  • Temporal transformer with two-way cross-attention
  • 16.3 M parameters

What it predicts

  • The correction to constant-velocity extrapolation — not the raw displacement.
  • So a model that learns nothing still beats naive by 41%.

How it is queried

  • DDIM sampler, 20 steps
  • Direct (k ≤ 3)
  • Rollout (10 frames)
  • 5 samples — their spread is the uncertainty estimate
10 videos · 7,371 windows · self-supervised throughout. Targets come from the reconstruction, never from an annotator. The method is written up in full in Section III of the IEEE manuscript, every hyperparameter stated.

Metrics chosen, and what they measured

The three questions we measure
  1. Is the predicted shape in the right place?Chamfer Distance
  2. Is the motion pointing the right way?Directional cosine
  3. Does the model know when it is unsure?Spread vs. realised error

The second exists because a shape score alone cannot tell “learned nothing” from “right direction, wrong distance”. They score the same.

What the answers were

Accuracy — not yet ahead

  • CD 0.01568 vs naive 0.01557. Constant velocity beats both, on all 10 videos.

Direction — clearly learned

  • Cosine +0.57 at t+1. Nothing-learned would read 0.00. So: magnitude, not direction.

Uncertainty — calibrated

  • Spread vs realised error, r = 0.69. No baseline provides this at all.
Two explanations tested and eliminated before we concluded anything. No drift (ρ = +0.02, p = 0.61) · no dropped frames (333 origins checked, 0 found). Six quality tests run as a suite; QT0 gates everything downstream.

Making the prediction visible

A prediction moves 512 points. A picture needs all 58,000. Bridging that gap is what turns a metric into something a surgeon could look at.

From points to pixels

  • Predicted motion of the 512 tracked points is carried onto every Gaussian by distance-weighted interpolation, so a tracked point receives its own motion exactly.

The renderer, re-verified

  • Module 2’s renderer was re-implemented here, including degree-3 spherical harmonics. We checked it against the original rather than assuming they matched.

Six panels per moment

  • Real frame, reconstruction ceiling, prediction, naive baseline, motion overlay and an amplified error map — with metrics over the full frame and tissue alone.

Predicted motion was invisible

  • At 1–2 pixels, predicted and real frames look identical. Motion is drawn as arrows coloured by agreement with truth and magnified automatically — the factor is printed on every panel, never applied to any reported number.

Nothing on screen was a prediction

  • Every predicted frame used to be paired with a frame that already existed. The forecast now runs 12 frames past the end of the recording: the real-video panel stops and says so, while the predicted panel keeps going.

Results 4 / 4 · our strongest result
Mid Evaluation · FYP-2

The model knows when it should not be trusted

r = 0.69spread vs actual error
5futures sampled per input
5×uncertainty growth over 10 frames

When the five sampled futures disagree, the prediction really is worse. Uncertainty rises smoothly with how far ahead we predict — 0.00011 at one frame, 0.00055 at ten.

Why this is the result we are proudest of

  • A system that is confidently wrong is unusable in theatre. A system that signals “do not trust me here” is usable even when imperfect.
  • No method in our comparison provides this.

Stated carefully

  • This is correlation between predicted spread and realised error. Some of it may reflect that busy moments are both harder and more variable — we say so in the paper rather than claiming more than we measured.

Module 5
Mid Evaluation · FYP-2

Module 5 — learning how tissue moves

A conditional diffusion model that learns the deformation of 512 tracked tissue points, trained entirely without clinical labels.

Input

  • Last 4 frames of 512 tracked points
  • How far ahead to predict (1, 2 or 3)
  • A patch of random noise to shape

Model

  • 16.3 M parameters
  • Temporal transformer per point
  • Two-way cross-attention
  • Denoises over 20 steps

Output

  • Movement of each point
  • Sample repeatedly → several plausible futures

Why diffusion and not direct regression

  • Soft tissue under contact is genuinely uncertain. A single estimate hides that. Sampling repeatedly gives a spread of outcomes — which Module 6 shows is a usable confidence signal.

Why the horizon is an input

  • The same 4 frames appear in training with targets 1, 2 and 3 frames ahead — three different right answers. Without being told which, the task is unlearnable no matter the capacity.
Built from 7,371 windows across 10 reconstructed videos — 5,910 train, 1,461 validation — with no human annotation at any stage.

Module 5 — what went wrong, and what we did

The task had no single right answerOne context appeared with targets 1, 2 and 3 frames ahead.
Condition on the horizonHorizon embedded and injected into both branches of the network.
The signal was smaller than the noiseTissue moves 0.004 units; diffusion adds noise at scale 1.
Rescale the target to unit varianceInverted before every reported metric, so results stay in physical units.
Raw trajectories had almost no momentumCorrelation between consecutive motions was 0.077 — nearly noise.
Adaptive temporal smoothingPer-video Savitzky–Golay window; correlation rose to 0.89.
Training made the model worse after epoch 60Held-out error rose while training loss kept falling.
Shorter schedule, stronger regularisation500 → 200 epochs, weight decay 0.01 → 0.05, best checkpoint kept.
A model that gave up scored the same as doing nothingPredicting zero displacement equalled the naive baseline.
Predict the correction to constant velocityNow giving up falls back to an estimator 41% better than naive.

Module 6
Mid Evaluation · FYP-2

Module 6 — using the model, and measuring it honestly

Module 6 trains nothing. It loads the trained model, asks it three different questions, and grades every answer against estimators that need no learning at all.

Ask once

  • Where will tissue be 1, 2 or 3 frames from now?
  • Most reliable mode.

Ask repeatedly

  • Feed each answer back in to reach 10 frames ahead.
  • Error compounds — measured.

Ask five times

  • Five possible futures from one input. How much they disagree is the confidence estimate.

Graded against two simple rules

  • · “Nothing moves” — the naive baseline
  • · “Everything continues straight” — constant velocity
  • If a 16 M-parameter model cannot beat these, we say so.

Six quality tests

  • QT0 verifies the evaluation data is the data the model trained on, before anything is predicted. QT1–QT5 cover accuracy, rollout, diversity, smoothness and cross-scene generalisation.
Module 6 is not there to make the model look good. It is there to establish, with evidence, exactly where the model stands.

Problems → solutions
Mid Evaluation · FYP-2

Module 6 — what went wrong, and what we did

Evaluation could have measured the wrong dataRollout needs continuous trajectories; Module 4 stores fixed windows.
Rebuild, then verify before predictingQT0 checks the rebuild against Module 4 output and runs as a gate.
The metric was penalising the model unfairlyChamfer Distance is minimised by the average, not by one sample.
Average several sampled trajectoriesRemoves a ~40% penalty the model was being charged for free.
Diagnostics were measuring one easy videoTaking the first N windows drew almost entirely from one recording.
Evenly spread the evaluation setEvery video contributes; earlier scores had been 4× too optimistic.
Our constant-acceleration baseline was wrongA backward difference gives the velocity at t − ½, not at t.
Derive the velocity at t properlyv = (3·x(t) − 4·x(t−1) + x(t−2)) / 2, verified exact on quadratic motion.
What we called a Kalman filter was not oneIt was an alpha–beta filter with fixed gains and no covariance.
Implement a real two-state Kalman filterFull 2×2 covariance; recovers a known velocity to 2.6e−06.

Motivation
Mid Evaluation · FYP-2

The laparoscope shows the present, never the next moment

A surgeon operates through one camera. It gives a flat image with no depth, no 3D structure, and no indication of how tissue will respond to the instrument about to touch it.

Reconstruction research has solved the first problem well. It has not addressed the second at all.

Every published surgical 4D reconstruction system is retrospective: it models deformation that has already been recorded.

What exists today

  • Reconstruct the scene in 3D, for frames already captured

What is missing

  • Anticipate where tissue will be a moment from now, with an honest indication of confidence

1–2 pxtissue motion per frame
0deformation labels in clinical video
